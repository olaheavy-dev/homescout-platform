# HomeScout: product research and recommended direction

**Research date:** 23 October 2026, Europe/London  
**Context:** Companion to `01-product-brief-v1.1.md`; the existing brief and all eight concept images were reviewed.  
**Confirmed by the founder:** Buyers and renters receive equal weight. **England first**: the first release covers English addresses only, while the UK-wide ambition and the existing technology direction remain the working context.  
**Precedence:** This document owns current product recommendations, decision status and geographic scope. `03-prototype-and-image-direction.md` owns proposed prototype behaviour and design within that scope; a design detail does not approve a product or commercial decision. Both supersede conflicting descriptions in the historical brief.  
**Decision status:** Recommendations for discussion, not an approved roadmap, validated demand, brand clearance or a description of working integrations. No MVP/post-MVP classification is introduced.

## 1. The recommended product

**A shared place to understand a home, test the cost of living there, and get important questions answered before committing.**

Keep the working promise: **Know the home before you commit.**

The central experience is a **home record** (the consumer term for the brief's "living property decision record"). A person brings an address, relevant documents and their household priorities. HomeScout connects the facts, estimates, observations and unanswered questions, then helps them compare alternatives and record what changed. The user remains the decision maker.

The proposed differentiation is the connection between **evidence → practical impact → question → answer → updated decision**. Maps, reports, calculations and AI explanations support that process. Each of those features separately has existing competitors.

This is a positioning hypothesis. Desk research supports the problem and establishes competitive overlap; only observation of real buyers and renters can establish whether this particular product earns repeat use or payment.

## 2. What the research establishes

| Finding | Evidence and limits | Product consequence |
|---|---|---|
| People encounter missing information and uncertainty late in a purchase | The government's June 2026 reform roadmap describes delayed, inconsistent upfront information and poor visibility of responsibility. It cites about 120 days after offer acceptance and roughly one-third of transactions falling through. These are the roadmap's estimates, not a universal UK benchmark. [Government roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/home-buying-and-selling-reform/outcome/home-buying-and-selling-reform-roadmap) | Show important unknowns before users spend more money; retain responsibility and next steps. Do not promise to prevent failed transactions. |
| First-time buyers struggle with the process as well as finding a home | A Lloyds-commissioned OnePoll survey of 2,000 prospective UK first-time buyers, conducted 17–21 July 2025, reports that 13% fully understood the buying process; legal steps were a prominent uncertainty. Bank-sponsored, self-reported research, not an assessment of all buyers. [Lloyds research](https://www.lloydsbank.com/mortgages/first-time-buyers/untangling-first-time-buyer-maze.html) | Explain why a finding matters and who can answer it. Avoid assuming the user understands conveyancing vocabulary. |
| Renting deserves a substantive product | England's Housing Survey estimates 1.8 million household moves in 2024–25, around half involving moves into or out of private renting. This is England, not UK market sizing, and not a count of potential paying customers. [EHS housing history](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/chapters-for-english-housing-survey-2024-to-2025-headline-findings-on-demographics-and-household-resilience/chapter-3-housing-history-and-future-housing) | Give renting its own onboarding, cost model, documents, questions and completed decision journey. |
| Service charges and management create real uncertainty | LEASE reports service charges as 30% of its completed enquiry mix in 2025/26. Its users are a selected advice-seeking population, not all leaseholders. Government buyer guidance explicitly asks buyers to investigate charge history and anticipated works. [LEASE insights](https://www.lease-advice.org/data-insights/), [government leasehold guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/buying-or-owning-a-leasehold-property/buying-or-owning-a-leasehold-home) | A good buyer scenario reconciles costs across documents and tracks the missing supporting evidence. |
| Public policy is moving toward more upfront information | The 2026 reform roadmap proposes standardised sales packs and further legislation. These are intentions and future work, not an already universal sales-pack requirement. [Government roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/home-buying-and-selling-reform/outcome/home-buying-and-selling-reform-roadmap) | Build around interpreting and updating evidence that may become more available. A business based only on finding currently hidden facts could become less distinctive. |

**Inference:** Buyers and renters share the need for clarity, comparison and accountable answers, but the timing and subject matter differ. The prototype should make both journeys feel native. Research has not established the best time to introduce the product, willingness to pay, or the frequency with which users can obtain useful replies.

### What can credibly distinguish the product

1. **A useful record of unresolved matters.** Each question has the evidence, requested answer, responsible party, reply and remaining uncertainty attached to the same home.
2. **Evidence that changes a household decision.** A corrected cost updates the comparison; a missing document remains visible at the next checkpoint.
3. **Equal quality across renting and buying.** Shared interaction patterns with different costs, documents, questions and local rules.
4. **A calm, comprehensible experience.** The user can identify the next useful action without learning a professional case-management system.

These are potential advantages in execution, not established exclusivity or a moat. A more credible long-term asset would be accurate property matching, permitted source access, a well-tested evidence model, and demonstrated user trust. Private customer documents must not become cross-customer training or retrieval material by default.

## 4. The two audiences, on equal terms

| Need | Buyer | Renter | Shared response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decide if it warrants more attention | Asking price, tenure, condition questions, initial costs | Rent, move-in timing, bills, tenancy and viewing questions | Concise home overview with explicit unknowns |
| Test real affordability | Deposit, borrowing assumptions, fees, taxes, service charges and maintenance | Rent, refundable deposit, first rent payment, overlap, moving costs, bills and travel | Editable recurring costs and cash needed at distinct dates |
| Understand daily life | Work, school, care, shopping and accessibility routines | The same, including housemate and shift-work routines where relevant | User-entered priorities, supported routing and clear evidence limits |
| Investigate documents | Survey, title/lease, management accounts, works information | Agreement/written terms, bills schedule, safety documents and inventory where relevant | Original document beside cited findings |
| Get answers | Agent, seller, conveyancer, surveyor, managing agent | Letting agent, landlord, relevant council or adviser | Questions, replies and evidence status |
| Make the choice | Before an offer and at later commitment checkpoints | Before payments/signing and before moving in | Comparison, remaining unknowns and a recorded household choice |

Buyer and renter legal processes vary within the UK. A checkpoint is a user journey concept, not a claim that every nation uses the same legal commitment sequence.

**Personas to test rather than assume:** a first-time buyer comparing two homes; a leasehold buyer checking charges; a renter comparing bills-inclusive and bills-exclusive homes; housemates agreeing priorities; a relocating household. Shared ownership, lodgers, student halls and other special cases need explicit routing rather than silently inheriting the ordinary private-tenancy path.

## 5. A coherent feature system

Use one global **My move** workspace. Each saved home holds its own investigation and documents. A household can compare homes within a buying journey or a renting journey; moving from renting to buying should not destroy the prior record.

### A. Understand this home

- Address search followed by explicit unit/flat confirmation when ambiguous.
- A concise overview: important findings, missing evidence and next useful actions.
- Source-backed property facts, energy, tenure where supported, sale history where available and relevant environmental context.
- Separate labels for property evidence, building evidence and area context.
- Source details and a way to correct a match or report a problem.
- A viewing companion with user notes, photos and the relevant unanswered questions.

### B. Test the real cost

- Monthly cash outgoings and upfront cash shown separately.
- Buyer assumptions cover borrowing, taxes/fees by jurisdiction, service charges and maintenance; renter assumptions cover bills, deposit, first payment, overlap and moving costs.
- Refundable deposits and mortgage principal must not be described as equivalent to consumption costs in a rent-versus-buy analysis.
- Deterministic calculations, editable assumptions, ranges for uncertain bills and explicit exclusions.
- Stress scenarios such as a different mortgage rate, revised service charge or higher utility usage, with visible input changes.
- Household split views may help housemates understand contributions, but must not imply they alter legal liability.

### C. Understand daily life

- Map and equivalent list for transport, shops, parks, childcare, schools, healthcare, worship and accessibility information.
- Optional destinations and time-of-day needs; do not force family, employment or income questions before showing value.
- Travel-time results need routing evidence; station proximity and stop locations are not commute reliability.
- School cards show the correct inspection framework/date and distinguish admissions information from an admissions promise.

### D. Compare trade-offs

- Two or three homes, with user-selected priorities and aligned assumptions.
- Plain statements such as “lower estimated monthly outgoings, longer modelled commute, service-charge budget still missing.”
- Missing data appears as missing; it must not become zero cost or a favourable score.
- A household can state non-negotiables and preferences separately. AI can explain the trade-off; it should not silently choose the weights.
- Personal preference mismatch, material financial impact and evidence uncertainty are separate concepts.

### E. Resolve questions

- Create a question from a finding, document excerpt, viewing observation or a blank form.
- Record who should answer and what would constitute adequate supporting evidence.
- Draft a request for the user to review, copy or export. A prototype must not imply that a message was actually sent.
- Attach the reply; distinguish a statement from its documentary support and the respondent's authority.
- Keep a partial answer open when it fails to answer the question.
- Record any updated cost, comparison or decision, preserving the previous version.

An AI interpretation of a reply is a proposed assessment. A user or explicitly identified reviewer records whether the requested evidence is adequate and why; do not silently close a question or describe a reply as professionally verified. A supported amount can be proposed as a new cost input, but applying it must be explicit, retain the prior value and identify every affected saved scenario. User overrides remain visible rather than being silently overwritten by later refreshes.

### F. Return and decide

- Surface changed evidence and overdue questions, prioritised by user relevance.
- Distinguish a source refresh from a changed property fact and from a changed user assumption.
- Share with explicit permissions; a shared overview need not expose private source documents.
- Produce a dated snapshot containing evidence, assumptions, open questions and the household's rationale.
- Allow a decision to be recorded with acknowledged uncertainties. “User chose to proceed” is not “property verified.”

AI belongs inside these tasks: explain a finding, compare documents, draft a question, interpret a reply and explain an updated scenario. The existing stack can support this, but the customer does not need a dashboard of named agents. "Agent-driven" describes how the product is **built** (by AI coding agents, see `04-project-goals-and-delivery.md`), not a requirement for a multi-agent runtime. Start with single, task-specific model calls that use tools and deterministic functions. Adopt agent orchestration only where a measured task needs it. The founder chose AI inside tasks **plus** an optional chat panel about the current home (decision 0004). The chat must cite evidence or say it doesn't know, use the cost engine for any number, and never call a home safe or verified.

## 6. Evidence design: make trust understandable

Use separate dimensions rather than a single confidence badge:

| Dimension | Example |
|---|---|
| Origin | Official dataset, uploaded document, listing claim, user observation, modelled estimate |
| Applicability | This flat, whole building, postcode, mapped area |
| Time | Effective period, observation date, publication date, last retrieved date |
| Status | Available, missing, conflicting, stale, not applicable |
| Interpretation | What HomeScout inferred, with its supporting evidence and limitations |

A source-backed fact is not automatically correct for the selected flat. Multiple agreeing sources do not establish independence. Fresh retrieval does not make an old certificate current. Missing evidence does not establish a defect, and low mapped risk does not establish absence of property risk.

Recommended question statuses: **To ask → Waiting for reply → Reply needs checking → Answer recorded**. Keep **Partially answered**, **Needs professional review**, **Closed by user without answer**, and **Reopened** available. Store the reason and evidence for each transition. Avoid an unexplained green “Resolved” checkmark.

Selecting “copy request” or exporting questions does not establish that a request was sent. Move to **Waiting for reply** only after the user records sending it or a future authorised communication integration confirms delivery state. A blocked or unresolved question can remain open when the user records a decision to proceed.

### England first, and why the other nations are not proportional effort

The first release covers England. Several of the sources above cover England only (Planning Data Platform, the long-term flood checker, the new Ofsted report cards, the English renting reforms), so England is also where the verified source base is strongest.

Adding each further nation is a separate piece of work, not a proportional increment:

| Nation | Candidate shared sources, subject to coverage and licence checks | Separate source/content work |
|---|---|---|
| Wales | HMLR Price Paid, the England/Wales EPC service, OS Open UPRN, NaPTAN, Ofcom, OSM | Occupation-contract tenancy journey and terminology, LTT instead of SDLT, Natural Resources Wales flood data, Welsh planning sources, Estyn school inspections, Welsh-language considerations |
| Scotland | OS Open UPRN, NaPTAN, Ofcom, OSM | Separate sales, title and EPC registers, private residential tenancy journey, LBTT, SEPA flood data, Scottish planning, Education Scotland inspections, and a different buying sequence (offers, missives) |
| Northern Ireland | Ofcom, OSM | Address and identity supply (OS Open UPRN and NaPTAN exclude NI), Land & Property Services, domestic rates instead of council tax, DfI flood data, NI tenancy rules and NI school inspection |

**Inference, not a cost estimate:** Wales has substantial candidate source overlap with England. That does not establish the cheapest expansion route: provider contracts, missing fields, localisation, legal-content review and validation costs have not been estimated. Scotland and Northern Ireland also require distinct sourcing and content work; the amount has not been quantified. Preserve a nation and jurisdiction field from the start and keep English rules out of shared components. Choose any later geographic sequence after supplier and effort assessment.

Until a nation is supported, an address there gets a clear "not yet covered" state. It should never get a partial result that could pass for a clean one.

### Four nations require different tenancy journeys

- **England:** official guidance describes the assured periodic tenancy changes effective 1 May 2026 for the relevant private-rented sector, with exceptions. Do not default a new ordinary English tenancy to a generic 12-month fixed term. [Tenant overview](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renters-rights-act-overview-for-tenants)
- **Wales:** use occupation-contract and written-statement terminology where applicable. [Welsh tenant guidance](https://www.gov.wales/renting-homes-frequently-asked-questions-tenants)
- **Scotland:** identify whether a private residential tenancy applies, rather than importing English contract terminology. [Scottish signing guidance](https://www.mygov.scot/tenant-new-tenancy/sign-agreement)
- **Northern Ireland:** include the locally relevant tenancy information and distinct services. [NI tenancy guidance](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/private-rent-and-tenancies)

The English private-rented-sector database was described as a forthcoming phased rollout in the October 2026 Commons briefing; it is not a currently complete nationwide lookup to depict as integrated. Recheck before implementing. [Commons implementation briefing](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10669/)

## 8. Commercial direction to test

Keep the brief's principle: useful public evidence, basic cost tools and a meaningful comparison are available free. Charging solely to reveal a material public-interest finding would undermine the trust proposition.

**Recommended hypothesis:** a time-limited **Move pass**, with a clearly stated duration, included document/investigation allowance, and a read-only retained record or export after expiry. Test this against a cancellable monthly plan and a one-off home investigation. Do not settle prices from competitor price points alone.

Neither the name Move pass nor its appearance in a prototype settles a plan or entitlement. The free/paid boundary for manual question recording, shared access, stored documents, assisted review, monitoring and exports remains to be specified. Expiry behaviour, retention/deletion rules and access to existing records must be defined together; the read-only/export suggestion above is a hypothesis, not an indefinite storage promise.

### Proposed free/paid boundary (draft for testing)

The free/paid plan still holds: free gives a complete, honest first look at any English home, and paid adds the depth that costs money to run. Draw the line by **marginal cost and depth, never by withholding a material public-interest fact**:

| Capability | Free | Paid (Move pass hypothesis) | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Address check, unit confirmation, home overview | ✓ | ✓ | Entry point and trust; mostly cached open data |
| Public evidence with sources, dates and coverage gaps (energy, sold prices, flood context, planning records) | ✓ | ✓ | Public-interest facts are never paywalled |
| Nearby map and list | ✓ (straight-line distances) | ✓ plus routed travel times to the household's own destinations | Routing is a metered third-party cost |
| Buy and rent cost calculators, one downside scenario | ✓ | ✓ plus multiple saved scenarios and stress sets | Deterministic and cheap; the free version must be honest |
| Compare homes | Up to 2 homes | Up to a larger limit, with household priorities | Comparison is core value; a limit, not a lock |
| Viewing notes and manual question list (record replies yourself) | ✓ | ✓ | The answer-tracking habit should form before payment |
| Document upload with AI extraction, cited findings and conflict detection | — | ✓ within a stated allowance | Model, storage and extraction cost |
| AI-drafted questions and AI reply checking | Limited sample | ✓ | Model cost; the user still records the outcome |
| Change monitoring and "what changed" explanations | — | ✓ during the pass | Repeated source checks and model cost |
| Household sharing with permissions | View-only link | ✓ shared editing | Collaboration is a strong paid signal in competitors |
| Dated decision snapshot and export | — | ✓ | Retained record is part of what the pass sells |
| After the pass expires | — | Read-only record and export for a stated period | Avoids holding user data hostage |

Allowances, limits, durations and prices are deliberately unset. Each paid row is a hypothesis for the user study in §10, and the boundary can move without changing the principle. In the portfolio build, entitlements are feature flags, and payment runs only in a payment provider's test mode until there is a commercial decision.

What could justify paying: repeated document reconciliation, a household workspace, answer tracking, material-change explanations and a usable record across multiple homes. Buyers and renters may value different allowances and durations; equal product weight does not require identical purchasing behaviour.

Research the economics before commitments: licensed address/data calls, routing, document extraction, model usage, storage, support, refunds and any professional review. A vendor's “from” price is not the cost of a complete consumer investigation. Maintain independence in commercial design; any referral revenue would require explicit disclosure and must not alter findings or ordering silently.

**Distribution hypotheses:** address-specific research content, viewing checklists, referrals between household members, and partnerships that preserve the user's control. Acquisition cost and retention are unknown. Do not assume search traffic or that a transaction-time product will produce year-round subscriptions.

## 10. Validate the product, not just its appearance

No user interviews or paid product tests were conducted in this research. A suitable next study is **six buyers and six renters**, selected to include different household structures and levels of moving experience. This is a proposed qualitative sample, not a statistically representative survey or a commercial milestone. Recruit for English moves first, matching the launch geography. Before extending to another nation, repeat the study with people moving there and get a professional review of that nation's examples.

Use recent real decisions and redacted materials with consent. First ask participants to demonstrate their existing process; do not introduce the proposed workflow as the correct answer.

Test whether each participant can:

1. Explain what is known about a home and identify a material unknown.
2. Distinguish property evidence from area context and a claimed cost from an estimate.
3. Compare two homes using their own priorities and realistic outgoings.
4. Turn a finding into a relevant question with an identifiable recipient.
5. Recognise that an incomplete reply has not answered the question.
6. Explain how a supported answer changes the cost or their decision.
7. Identify why they would return and whether they would pay for that specific value.

Measure task completion, unsupported conclusions, time to identify a material gap, meaningful answer capture and return use in a real move. Record confidence separately from accuracy: a polished product that merely increases confidence is not a successful outcome.

Reconsider the direction if users mainly want a disposable report, rarely obtain replies, find the workflow burdensome, or cannot distinguish its benefit from their current tools. Preserve the free-investigation value even if paid answer tracking proves weak.

## 11. Research boundaries

- Source dates and geography are kept beside claims. No attempt is made to convert England-only evidence into UK totals.
- Competitor marketing establishes advertised overlap, not product quality, subscriber numbers or defensibility.
